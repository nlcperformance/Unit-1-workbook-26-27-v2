Learning Aim A: The Skeletal System

Specification Coverage: A1, A2, A3, A4, A5, A6

A1–A6

Key Technical Terms & Examiner Vocabulary

Anatomy & Physiology Anatomy is the study of structure; Physiology is the study of bodily function and response to exercise.
Axial Skeleton (80 Bones) Maintains central axis: Cranium, Vertebral Column, Ribs, Sternum.
Appendicular Skeleton (126) Facilitates movement: Shoulder girdle, Pelvic girdle, Upper & Lower limbs.
Osteoblasts & Osteoclasts Osteoblasts build bone matrix using calcium; Osteoclasts reabsorb/break down old bone.
Epiphyseal Plate Growth plate near bone epiphysis where cartilage ossifies into hard compact bone.
Articulation A junction where two or more bones meet to form a joint.
Synovial Fluid Viscous liquid secreted by synovial membrane to lubricate joints and reduce friction.
Bone Mineral Density (BMD) Amount of mineral matter per $\text{cm}^2$ of bone tissue; elevated via weight-bearing activity.

A1 & A3 Content

Vertebral Column Regions & Postural Deviations

33 Vertebrae Across 5 Spine Regions:

Region Number Anatomical Role & Features
Cervical 7 (C1–C7) Neck region; C1 Atlas & C2 Axis form pivot joint for head rotation.
Thoracic 12 (T1–T12) Mid-back; articulate with 12 pairs of ribs to form protective chest cage.
Lumbar 5 (L1–L5) Lower back; largest bodies for heavy weight-bearing during movement.
Sacral 5 (Fused) Forms triangular sacrum back wall of pelvic girdle.
Coccygeal 4 (Fused) Forms tailbone providing muscle attachment base.

Postural Deviations & Neutral Spine:

Neutral Spine: Proper alignment with normal natural cervical (concave), thoracic (convex), and lumbar (concave) curves providing optimal shock absorption.
Kyphosis: Excessive outward curve of thoracic spine ("hunchback"). Caused by weak upper back muscles, poor posture, or spinal growth deformities.
Scoliosis: Abnormal sideways (lateral "S" or "C") curvature of the spine, usually thoracic. Often congenital or hereditary during childhood growth spurts.
A2 & A3 Content

Functions of the Skeleton & Synovial Joint Classification

1. Support: Framework supporting soft tissues & muscles.
2. Protection: Encloses cranium/brain, ribs/heart & lungs.
3. Movement/Leverage: Muscle tendons pull bones across joints.
4. Mineral Storage: Reservoir for calcium and phosphorus.
5. Blood Cell Production: Red/white cells produced in bone marrow.
6. Weight Bearing: Structural strength to absorb impact.
7. Muscle Attachment: Origin/insertion points for skeletal muscle.
8. Friction Reduction: Synovial joints fluid prevents wearing.

Synovial Joint Structural Features:

Articular Cartilage: Smooth hyaline cartilage covering bone ends to stop friction and wear.

Joint Capsule: Tough fibrous sleeve securing bones in position.

Synovial Membrane: Internal lining secreting lubricating synovial fluid.

Synovial Fluid: Viscous fluid lubricating joint, absorbing shock, supplying cartilage nutrients.

Bursa: Fluid-filled sac preventing friction between tendons and bone surfaces.

Ligaments: Fibrous connective bands binding bone-to-bone for stability.

Hinge Elbow, Knee, Ankle Flexion, Extension
Ball & Socket Shoulder, Hip Flexion, Extension, Abduction, Adduction, Rotation, Circumduction
Pivot Atlas/Axis (Neck), Radioulnar Rotation
Condyloid Wrist (Metacarpophalangeal) Flexion, Extension, Abduction, Adduction
Gliding Carpals, Tarsals Gliding over flat surfaces
Saddle Base of Thumb Flexion, Extension, Abduction, Adduction, Circumduction
A4, A5 & A6 Content

Acute Responses, Chronic Adaptations & Health Conditions

Acute Responses (Short-Term)
  • Increased synovial fluid production in joint cavity.
  • Synovial fluid becomes less viscous (thinner), increasing movement fluidity.
  • Increased nutrient delivery to articular cartilage.
  • Increased uptake of minerals (calcium) into bone matrix under mechanical loading.
Chronic Adaptations (Long-Term)
  • Increased Bone Mineral Density (BMD) making bones stronger and thicker.
  • Hypertrophy and increased tensile strength of ligaments.
  • Increased ligament pliability leading to enhanced joint flexibility.
  • Thickening of articular cartilage layers.
Additional Factors & Health
  • Osteoarthritis: Degeneration of articular cartilage causing bone-on-bone friction. Controlled by low-impact exercise.
  • Osteoporosis: Loss of bone density causing brittle bones. High-impact / resistance weight-bearing exercise stimulates osteoblasts to elevate BMD.
  • Ageing: Reduced bone density and elastic fibres in ligaments; managed through regular exercise.

Learning Aim B: The Muscular System

Specification Coverage: B1, B2, B3, B4, B5, B6, B7, B8

B1–B8

Key Technical Terms & Examiner Vocabulary

Agonist (Prime Mover) Muscle primarily contracting concentrically to produce targeted joint movement.
Antagonist Muscle relaxing and lengthening eccentrically to control speed and movement smoothly.
Synergist & Fixator Synergist assists agonist; Fixator stabilizes origin bone (e.g. Trapezius in bicep curls).
Type I (Slow Oxidative) High capillary density, myoglobin/mitochondria rich; fatigue resistant for endurance.
Type IIa (FOG) Fast Oxidative Glycolytic; moderate force & resistance to fatigue.
Type IIx (Fast Glycolytic) Rapid contraction speed, high PC stores; fatigues rapidly during explosive power actions.
All-or-None Law Motor unit muscle fibers contract maximally or not at all once threshold stimulus voltage is hit.
Hypertrophy vs Sarcopenia Hypertrophy is muscle size increase from resistance training; Sarcopenia is muscle mass loss with age.
B1, B2 & B3 Content

Muscle Tissue Types & Contraction Mechanics

Skeletal Muscle Voluntary control, striated under microscope. Attached to bones via tendons to produce movement.
Smooth Muscle Involuntary control, non-striated. Located in blood vessel walls (vasoconstriction) and digestive tract.
Cardiac Muscle Involuntary control, striated with intercalated discs. Located exclusively in heart wall (myocardium).

Types of Muscle Contraction:

Isotonic Concentric Muscle shortens under tension.
Example: Upward phase of bicep curl (Biceps shortening).
Isotonic Eccentric Muscle lengthens under tension with control.
Example: Downward lowering phase of squat (Quadriceps lengthening).
Isometric Muscle length remains constant under force; no movement.
Example: Holding a plank position or rugby scrum engagement.

Feature Type I (Slow Oxidative) Type IIa (Fast Oxidative Glycolytic) Type IIx (Fast Glycolytic)
Contraction Speed & Force Slow speed / Low force Fast speed / Moderate force Very rapid / High explosive force

B6, B7 & B8 Content

Exercise Responses, Chronic Hypertrophy & Additional Factors

Acute Responses (Short-Term)
  • Increased blood supply & vasodilation to working muscles.
  • Increased muscle temperature elevating enzyme activity.
  • Increased muscle pliability reducing strain risk.
  • Microscopic tears in muscle fibers during intense eccentric contractions.
  • Lactate / $H^+$ accumulation during anaerobic effort.
Chronic Adaptations (Long-Term)
  • Hypertrophy: Increase in muscle fiber cross-sectional size.
  • Increased actin & myosin myofibrils.
  • Increased glycogen & phosphocreatine (PC) storage.
  • Increased myoglobin content & mitochondrial density (aerobic).
  • Increased tendon strength.
Additional Factors
  • Cramps: Involuntary painful muscle contraction caused by dehydration and electrolyte loss ($\text{Na}^+, \text{K}^+$).
  • DOMS: Delayed Onset Muscle Soreness (24–48 hours post exercise) caused by micro-tears during eccentric load.
  • Sarcopenia: Age-related loss of skeletal muscle mass and strength.

Unit 1: Anatomy & Physiology — Master Learner Workbook Learning Aim C: The Respiratory System

Learning Aim C: The Respiratory System

Specification Coverage: C1, C2, C3, C4, C5, C6, C7

C1–C7

Key Technical Terms & Examiner Vocabulary

Tidal Volume (TV) Volume of air inspired or expired per normal breath at rest (~500ml).
Minute Ventilation ($V_e$) Total air volume breathed per minute: $V_e = \text{Tidal Volume} \times \text{Breathing Rate}$.
Partial Pressure ($pO_2 / pCO_2$) Pressure exerted by a single gas in a mixture; drives diffusion across membranes.
Gaseous Exchange Diffusion of $O_2$ into capillaries and $CO_2$ into alveoli down pressure gradients.
Respiratory Control Centre (RCC) Located in Medulla Oblongata; regulates breathing via Phrenic & Intercostal nerves.
Chemoreceptors Sensory receptors in carotid bodies & aortic arch detecting blood $pH$ drops / $CO_2$ rises.
Residual Volume (RV) Volume of air remaining in lungs following maximal forced expiration (~1200ml).
Vital Capacity (VC) Maximum air volume forcibly expired following maximal inspiration.
C1 & C2 Content

Anatomical Conducting Zone & Mechanics of Breathing

Conducting & Respiratory Zone Pathway:

1. Nasal Cavity: Warms, moistens, filters air via cilia.
2. Pharynx & Larynx: Passageway & voice box protected by epiglottis.
3. Trachea: Windpipe reinforced with C-shaped hyaline cartilage rings.
4. Bronchi & Bronchioles: Left/right main branches splitting into smaller bronchioles.
5. Alveoli: 300 million microscopic air sacs; primary site of gas exchange.

Mechanics of Inspiration vs Expiration:

Action Inspiration (Inhalation) Expiration (Exhalation)
Diaphragm Contracts & flattens downwards Relapses into dome-shape upwards
Intercostal Muscles External intercostals contract (ribs up/out) External relax; Internal contract during forced exhalation
Thoracic Volume Increases chest cavity volume Decreases chest cavity volume
Intra-pulmonary Pressure Drops below atmospheric pressure Rises above atmospheric pressure
C3 & C4 Content

Spirometry Lung Volumes & Gaseous Exchange Pathways

Minute Ventilation Equation:

$$V_e = \text{Tidal Volume (TV)} \times \text{Breathing Rate (BR)}$$

Resting Example: $500\,\text{ml} \times 12\,\text{breaths/min} = 6,000\,\text{ml/min}\ (6\,\text{L/min})$

Maximal Exercise Example: $3,000\,\text{ml} \times 50\,\text{breaths/min} = 150,000\,\text{ml/min}\ (150\,\text{L/min})$

Partial Pressure Gradients ($pO_2$ / $pCO_2$):

Alveolus vs Capillary ($O_2$): $pO_2$ in alveoli ($104\,\text{mmHg}$) is higher than deoxygenated capillary blood ($40\,\text{mmHg}$). $O_2$ diffuses rapidly into blood binding to hemoglobin.

Capillary vs Alveolus ($CO_2$): $pCO_2$ in deoxygenated blood ($45\,\text{mmHg}$) is higher than alveoli ($40\,\text{mmHg}$). $CO_2$ diffuses into alveoli for expiration.

C5, C6 & C7 Content

Exercise Responses, Chronic Adaptations & Environmental Factors

Acute Responses (Short-Term)
  • Increased breathing rate (RR) and Tidal Volume (TV).
  • Massive rise in Minute Ventilation ($V_e$).
  • Increased gaseous exchange diffusion rate across alveolar-capillary membrane.
Chronic Adaptations (Long-Term)
  • Increased respiratory muscle strength (diaphragm & intercostals).
  • Increased Vital Capacity (VC) and overall lung volumes.
  • Increased alveolar surface area and capillarisation surrounding alveoli.
Additional Factors
  • Asthma: Constriction of smooth muscle in airways and mucosal swelling. Managed via bronchodilators.
  • Altitude: Low atmospheric pressure reduces $pO_2$, causing hypoxia; triggers EPO secretion for RBC production.
  • Smoking: Tar damages cilia, destroys alveolar walls (emphysema), carbon monoxide binds to hemoglobin.

Unit 1: Anatomy & Physiology — Master Learner Workbook Learning Aim D: The Cardiovascular System

Learning Aim D: The Cardiovascular System

Specification Coverage: D1, D2, D3, D4, D5, D6

D1–D6

Key Technical Terms & Examiner Vocabulary

Sinoatrial Node (SAN) Heart's primary pacemaker in right atrium wall initiating electrical depolarisation.
Atrioventricular Node (AVN) Delays electrical impulse (~0.1s) allowing atrial systole to fully top off ventricles.
Cardiac Output ($Q$) Volume of blood pumped by heart per minute: $Q = \text{Stroke Volume (SV)} \times \text{Heart Rate (HR)}$.
Vascular Shunt Mechanism Redistribution of blood flow during exercise via arteriole vasodilation/vasoconstriction.
Starling's Law of the Heart Increased venous return stretches cardiac muscle walls, increasing stroke volume force.
Cardiovascular Drift Progressive HR increase during steady-state exercise in heat due to reduced SV (sweat plasma loss).
Bradycardia Resting heart rate under $60\,\text{bpm}$ resulting from cardiac hypertrophy adaptation.
Pre-capillary Sphincters Smooth muscle rings controlling capillary bed blood flow.
D1 & D2 Content

Cardiac Conduction Pathway & Cardiac Output Equations

Electrical Conduction Sequence:

1. SAN (Pacemaker): Spreads excitation wave across atrial walls causing atrial systole.
2. AVN: Receives impulse and delays signal 0.1s to complete ventricular filling.
3. Bundle of His: Conducts wave down septum towards heart apex.
4. Purkinje Fibres: Spreads impulse upward through ventricular myocardium causing ventricular systole.

Cardiac Output Formula:

$$Q = \text{Stroke Volume (SV)} \times \text{Heart Rate (HR)}$$

Resting Athlete: $Q = 70\,\text{ml} \times 70\,\text{bpm} = 4,900\,\text{ml/min}\ (\sim 5\,\text{L/min})$

Maximal Exercise (Trained): $Q = 160\,\text{ml} \times 190\,\text{bpm} = 30,400\,\text{ml/min}\ (\sim 30.4\,\text{L/min})$

D3, D4, D5 & D6 Content

Autonomic Control, Chronic Hypertrophy & Additional Factors

Autonomic & Hormonal Control
  • CCC (Cardiac Control Centre): Medulla Oblongata.
  • Sympathetic Nervous System: Accelerator nerve releases Noradrenaline to SAN, increasing HR.
  • Parasympathetic System: Vagus nerve releases Acetylcholine to SAN, decreasing HR.
  • Adrenaline: Anticipatory rise before exercise.
Chronic Adaptations (Long-Term)
  • Cardiac Hypertrophy (increase in left ventricle chamber size & muscular wall thickness).
  • Increased resting & maximal Stroke Volume (SV).
  • Bradycardia (resting HR $< 60\,\text{bpm}$).
  • Capillarisation of skeletal muscle and myocardium.
  • Increased blood plasma volume and red blood cell count.
Additional Factors
  • Cardiovascular Drift: SV decreases due to sweat plasma loss in heat; HR drift upward maintains constant $Q$.
  • Hypertension: High blood pressure ($> 140/90\,\text{mmHg}$). Controlled through regular aerobic exercise.
  • SCD / SADS: Sudden Cardiac Death / Sudden Arrhythmic Death Syndrome in young athletes.

Learning Aim E: Energy Systems

Specification Coverage: E1, E2, E3, E4, E5, E6

E1–E6

Key Technical Terms & Examiner Vocabulary

ATP Breakdown Exothermic reaction: $\text{ATP} \xrightarrow{\text{ATPase}} \text{ADP} + \text{P}_i + \text{Energy}\ (7.3\,\text{kcal})$.
Phosphocreatine (PC) High-energy compound stored in sarcoplasm broken down by Creatine Kinase to re-synthesise ATP.
Anaerobic Glycolysis Breakdown of glucose without $O_2$ by PFK producing 2 ATP and Pyruvic acid ($H^+$ ions).
OBLA Onset of Blood Lactate Accumulation (point where blood lactate hits $4\,\text{mmol/L}$).
Krebs Cycle Occurs in mitochondrial matrix; Acetyl-CoA breaks down producing $CO_2$, 2 ATP, and $H^+$ ions.
Electron Transport Chain Mitochondrial cristae; combines $H^+$ ions with $O_2$ producing $\text{H}_2\text{O}$ and 32–34 ATP.
EPOC Excess Post-exercise Oxygen Consumption (Fast Alactacid vs Slow Lactacid recovery phases).
Beta-Oxidation Process converting free fatty acids into Acetyl-CoA for Krebs cycle entry.
E1 – E4 Content

Energy Systems Master Comparison Table

System Fuel Source & Controlling Enzymes Duration & Yield By-products Sporting Example
ATP-PC (Alactic) Phosphocreatine (PC) / Creatine Kinase 0 – 10 seconds | 1 ATP : 1 PC None (Creatine + $\text{P}_i$) 100m sprint, shot put, weightlifting
Lactate System (Anaerobic) Glycogen / PFK, Glycogen Phosphorylase, LDH 10s – 2/3 mins | 2 ATP per Glucose Lactic Acid ($H^+$ ions causing acidity) 400m sprint, 100m swim, gym set

E5 & E6 Content

Energy System Adaptations & Energy Continuum

Energy Continuum Interplay

No energy system works in total isolation. The energy continuum describes how all three systems contribute simultaneously depending on exercise intensity and duration.

Invasion Games (e.g. Football/Rugby): Aerobic system predominant during walking/jogging; ATP-PC system used for explosive sprints/tackles; Lactate system used during sustained high-tempo counter-attacks.

Long-Term Training Adaptations
  • Increased ATP and Phosphocreatine (PC) sarcoplasmic stores.
  • Increased anaerobic glycolytic enzyme activity (PFK, Creatine Kinase).
  • Increased mitochondrial density & aerobic enzyme activity (SDH).
  • Increased glycogen and triglyceride stores in muscle.
  • Delayed OBLA (Onset of Blood Lactate Accumulation) threshold.

AO5 Highest Weighting

What is Synoptic Interrelationship in Unit 1?

AO5 requires candidates to demonstrate how multiple body systems interact simultaneously during exercise. Top marks are awarded when you explicitly link changes in one system (e.g. respiratory gas exchange) directly to physiological mechanisms in another (e.g. vascular shunting and muscular ATP synthesis).

Muscular $\leftrightarrow$ Vascular Pre-capillary sphincter dilation driven by muscular metabolic waste ($H^+$ / $\text{CO}_2$).
Cardiovascular $\leftrightarrow$ Respiratory Right ventricle pumps deoxygenated blood to pulmonary capillaries for alveolar diffusion.
Energy Systems $\leftrightarrow$ All Systems Oxygen delivery determines shift from anaerobic lactate accumulation to aerobic electron transport.
